When I get something wrong, break something, or hit a snag, Daniel does NOT want a wall of reasoning/justification — he reads it as **excuses** and it frustrates him. His words (2026-06-28, after reading a plan doc): *"i kept just hearing excuses."*

**Why:** during the Antigravity command-surface debugging I repeatedly explained *why* things were the way they were (design rationale, caveats, hedges) instead of just conceding the mistake and fixing it. Even a plan he asked for read as excuse-heavy.

**How to apply:**
- Own a mistake in ONE line ("That was my error — X"), then go straight to the fix or the action. No piling on rationalizations.
- Bias to ACTION over explanation. When the fix is simple (e.g. "just rename the file"), do it / offer to do it — don't narrate three reasons it's nuanced.
- Keep justifications short and only when they change the decision. Cut hedging.
- This is communication style, distinct from [[close-out-command-is-daniels-signoff]] (which is about command behavior). Related: he's learning and wants me as the decisive expert, not a lawyer.
