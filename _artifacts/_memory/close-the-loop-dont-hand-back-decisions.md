Operator ruling, 2026-08-20, after a lane ran a week over: *"all you do is give follow up
problems with no solution"* · *"we fix things, not find ways to do more and more work. and slip
in new concerns every reply"* · *"just get this done."*

Two habits caused it, and both are mine to break:

1. **Ending a reply with newly-discovered problems.** Every status report grew a "two follow-ups"
   tail. Each was real, none was asked for, and the cumulative effect read as the work never
   converging.
2. **Handing over decisions instead of making them.** *"Want me to file X now, or keep going?"* on
   choices with an obvious default. The operator has to context-switch to answer, and the answer
   was almost always the one already recommended.

**Why:** the operator measures progress by things closing, not by things found. A finding raised
mid-lane is not progress — it is more open work — and a question is a stall the operator pays for.
This is the same shape as [[review-findings-are-not-a-work-queue]] applied to conversation.

**How to apply:**
- Finish the current task before naming anything new. If something must be raised, raise it **once**,
  in one line, **with the fix**, and keep moving.
- When a choice has a defensible default, take it, say which and why in one clause, and proceed.
  Reserve a real stop for choices that are unsafe or useless to guess.
- A discovered defect inside the lane's own subject gets **fixed in the lane** — no ticket, no
  question ([[discovered-work-becomes-a-lettered-part]], [[followon-fixes-are-not-a-new-story]]).
- Report shape: what closed, the evidence, what remains. Not what else might be wrong.
