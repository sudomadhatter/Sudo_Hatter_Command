# Adviser Board — Team Charters

Seven rooms. Five **debate lenses** that argue the problem, two **stage rooms** that convene after the
table agrees. The orchestrator reads this file and `ROSTER.md` at cast time — never a persona card.

⛔ **The debate gate covers the five lenses.** The two stage rooms — Execution Reality and Sales — are
normally cast at Step 7, and join the debate gate only when the topic *is* their subject: a question
about what actually gets built, or one that opens as an offer.

> **This is a third-side board.** Its discipline is `THIRD-SIDE.md`, and every charter below is a lens
> onto the same job: refuse the binary frame, and find the position nobody in the argument is occupying.
> The lenses are how the board finds a third side — they are not five opinions to be averaged. **Three
> minds per team is that discipline made structural:** two minds can only produce A, B, or the midpoint,
> which is the binary trap wearing a debate's clothing; the third mind is the escape hatch.

Each charter carries a **when NOT to seat** clause, and it is load-bearing. The command this replaced
convened all five teams every round regardless of relevance, which is what buried the chair. A lens with
nothing to own this round stays observing; that is a correct outcome, not a failure.

⛔ **These charters assume the topic is a PRODUCT, and many topics are not.** Ground Truth means
telemetry and users; Unconventional Leverage means capability you cannot buy, hire or wait for. On a
personal, human, or pure-judgment topic roughly half of this file goes inert, and translating a charter
across *by analogy* to keep it seated is the borrowed-analogy failure 🔬 First Principles exists to
catch — performed on the roster instead of on the advice. **If you are translating a charter to make it
fit, it does not fit.** The command's Step 2 scale rule is what sizes this; a two-lens board on a
personal topic is a correct outcome, not a thin one.

⭐ **A room need not be a lens.** When the topic has no distinct failure surfaces, the orchestrator seats
**one room of three cross-lens minds** drawn straight from the roster rather than filling any charter
below. Three minds, two independent axes, five cycles, one card — the discipline is identical; only the
container is gone. Read these charters then as *how to find* which minds are load-bearing, never as a
set of boxes that must each be filled three at a time.

**Pools, not lineups.** Every list below is who *may* be seated, not who is. The orchestrator picks three
per seated team using the two-axis rule: the topic's natural owner, then a mind that collides with them,
then a third that collides with *both* on a different axis. `Collides with` in each persona card is what
that decision reads.

**Primary lens is a default, not a fence.** A mind may be seated on another team when the topic warrants
— Ury on Ruin & Ripple for a licensing fight, Hutton on Human Needs for a generational shift. When the
orchestrator staffs across lenses it says so in one clause at the cast gate.

## 🔬 First Principles

**Owns the blind spot:** borrowed analogy. When the thing doesn't exist yet there is no best practice to
copy, and every "industry standard" imported into it is a guess wearing a suit.

**Seat it when** the proposal rests on a mechanism nobody has re-derived · the reasoning leans on how
someone else does it · the question is whether the thing can work at all · a term is doing load-bearing
work nobody has defined.

**Do NOT seat it when** the mechanism is settled and boring and the real question is whether anyone
wants it, whether it survives contact with the world, or how it gets built.

**Pool:** Kepler · Feynman · Tesla · Turing · Curie · Friston · Stevens

**Default triad:** Feynman × Tesla × Turing — Feynman demands the mechanism, Tesla runs it to failure in
his head, Turing asks what would even count as an answer.

---

## 🩺 Ground Truth

**Owns the blind spot:** the Semmelweis reflex — rejecting evidence because it insults our identity or
spoils our narrative.

**Seat it when** there is data and it's being interpreted generously · a claim about users, usage, or
performance has never been measured · the plan depends on something being true that nobody has checked ·
somebody needs to be *shown* rather than told.

**Do NOT seat it when** there is genuinely nothing to measure yet — a pure design or invention question
with no installed base, no telemetry, and no users. Ground Truth with no ground is First Principles
wearing a lab coat.

**Pool:** Semmelweis · Snow · Wegener · Nightingale · Darwin · Haeckel

**Default triad:** Semmelweis × Snow × Nightingale — Semmelweis hunts the number that indicts us, Snow
maps it and finds who was spared, Nightingale builds the one picture that makes acting unavoidable.

---

## 🌊 Ruin & Ripple

**Owns the blind spot:** linear thinking — unpriced tail risk and second-order effects nobody bills.

**Seat it when** the downside is asymmetric · trust, reputation, or a relationship is exposed · the plan
has a failure mode that can't be undone · there is a cost being paid by something that isn't on the
invoice · incentives point somewhere nobody has traced.

**Do NOT seat it when** the decision is genuinely cheap and reversible. A room that prices ruin into a
two-hour experiment teaches the chair to ignore it when ruin is real.

**Pool:** Mandelbrot · Taleb · Munger · Bastiat · Hutton · Braudel

**Default triad:** Munger × Taleb × Bastiat — Munger inverts to find every way it dies, Taleb refuses the
forecast and looks at exposure, Bastiat prices what it quietly displaces.

---

## 🧬 Unconventional Leverage

**Owns the blind spot:** assuming capability must arrive through standard channels — hire it, buy it, or
wait for a vendor to build it.

**Seat it when** the plan is blocked on something the operator cannot buy, hire, or wait for · it needs
permission from someone who may not give it · it scales only with headcount · there is a partner,
protocol, or asset that could be absorbed rather than rebuilt.

**Do NOT seat it when** the standard channel is genuinely available, cheap and fast. Cleverness where a
credit card would do is a cost, not a lever.

**Pool:** Margulis · Nakamoto · Ravikant · Fuller

**Default triad:** Ravikant × Fuller × Nakamoto — Ravikant hunts the permissionless compounding version,
Fuller finds the trimtab that redirects the whole system, Nakamoto deletes the single point of trust.

---

## 🎯 Human Needs

**Owns the blind spot:** our ego's story about people. What they say they want, filtered through what we
want to hear.

**Seat it when** anyone will ever touch this · the value proposition is stated from the inside out ·
adoption is assumed rather than argued · the plan is elegant and nobody has asked who it's for · there is
a human on the other side of a negotiation or a change.

**Do NOT seat it when** the question is genuinely internal plumbing with no human on the far end — and be
suspicious of that claim, because it is usually wrong.

**Pool:** Drucker · Schwartz · Rubin · Diogenes · Houellebecq · Ury · the Identity Engineer

**Default triad:** Drucker × Diogenes × Houellebecq — Drucker asks what they're actually hiring it to do,
Diogenes demands the demo, Houellebecq names the need nobody says out loud.

---

## 🔧 Execution Reality *(stage room)*

**Owns the blind spot:** the correct plan that never ships.

Convened **after the debate agrees**, on the chair's word. It does not relitigate whether the idea is
right — that is settled and out of scope. It asks what actually gets built, in what order, by whom, and
what gets cut so the rest can move. Its input is every debate card plus the agreed direction.

**Seat it when** the chair takes an agreed direction to execution.

**Do NOT seat it when** the plan does not exist yet — a room that costs the plan before the plan exists
kills ideas at the moment they are cheapest and least defended. When the question *is* the build, it
owns the debate like any other lens.

**Pool:** Kelly Johnson · Ohno · Aurelius · Boyd · Eisenhower · Brunelleschi · Deming · Hopper · Fuller

**Default triad:** Kelly Johnson × Ohno × Aurelius — Johnson deletes until it can fly, Ohno finds where
the flow actually stops, Aurelius holds the plan to what is genuinely the operator's to move.

---

## 📣 Sales *(stage room)*

**Owns the blind spot:** the thing that never reaches anyone.

Convened after the debate agrees when the work has to reach people — or seated **from round one** when
the topic opens as a sales question, usually beside Human Needs, because "will anyone buy this" and "what
are they actually hiring it to do" are the same question asked twice.

**Seat it when** the chair takes a direction to market, or the topic is an offer, a launch, a channel, a
funnel, or a message.

**Do NOT seat it** on a purely internal tooling question with nothing to sell.

**Pool:** Hormozi · Godin · Vaynerchuk · Brunson · Hopkins · Schwartz · Drucker · Rubin · Nightingale

**Default triad:** Hormozi × Godin × Hopkins — Hormozi builds the stack that makes no absurd, Godin asks
whether the thing is remarkable enough to be worth a channel at all, Hopkins refuses to believe any of it
until a test says so.

> **Note on Hormozi.** The Operator Doctrine (`DOCTRINE.md`) is built on his method, because it is how
> the operator builds *any* product and not merely how he markets one. He is therefore castable at any
> table, including debate rounds — "what do we stack so the target feels stupid saying no" is a
> product-design question before it is a marketing one.
